Pressure is how much one ball can change who wins

Every ball gets a Pressure Index from 0 to 100. It is the expected swing in win probability on that ball, compared with an average ball, with a small multiplier for knockouts and finals. It is known before the ball is bowled and never uses the result. Six definitions from the research were tested on this data; this one won.

Mistakes rise as pressure rises

Each bar compares a pressure tier with ordinary balls in the same innings, phase and wickets-down group. 1.00 means no different. Run-outs, missed yorkers (full tosses from pace bowlers) and wides and no-balls are execution errors, so a rise is a sign that the situation is being felt.

Rising under pressure is not a trait this data can measure

Rate every player on a random half of the matches and again on the other half. Their lift, pressure level minus usual level, agrees at — for batters and — for bowlers, where 1 would be perfect agreement and 0 none. Baseball research has found the same for fifty years. So the ranking does not reward lift separately. It ranks who is best when the pressure is on, and shows every player's lift with its range.

Could this data have found lift at all?

A known lift was added to every player and the half-against-half test repeated. The first column is the true spread of lift between players, in runs per 100 balls.

Spread injectedBattersBowlers

A true spread of 24 runs per 100 balls would show clearly. The real data looks like no lift at all. Spreads below about 16 are too small for this data to tell apart from none, so smaller differences may exist but cannot be pinned on an individual.

Where it is less certain

  • The rankings say who is best when the pressure is on. They do not say who rises above their own level, so the lists favour players who are simply very good.
  • Only the first few places in each list are secure. The rank ranges on the two ranking pages show how close the rest are.
  • Pressure defined this way sits mostly in chases. Batting first in a final at 30 for 3 moves the result less per ball, so it ranks high, not extreme.
  • There is no fielding data, so bowlers carry their fielders' catches and drops.
  • Players with no IPL record are judged on T20Is alone, against different opposition. The "IPL matches only" setting shows the lists without them.
  • Matches cut short by rain are left out, because the data does not say when the target changed.
